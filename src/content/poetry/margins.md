Here at the edge of the argument
I have written: *no*.

Not because the argument is wrong —
it may be right, or close to right —
but because the margin
is the space left for dissent,
and I intend to use it.

---

My great-aunt annotated everything.
Her copies of novels from the fifties
are thick with small pencil marks:
a question here, a bracket there,
a row of dots beside a passage
whose significance she never explained.

I have inherited the habit.
My books are conversations.
The books I bought new
and read in careful silence,
keeping myself away from them —
those are the ones I don't remember.

---

The margin is not supplementary.
It is where the text
comes up against the limit
of what it could contain.

It is where the reader enters
not as audience
but as respondent,
not as the person to whom something is said
but as the one who has something to say.

---

I have a theory about knowledge:
it lives in the margin.
Not in the official text,
the settled argument,
the position defended —

but in the note someone wrote
to themselves at three in the morning,
the small *yes* or *wait*
or *but what about—*

Knowledge as negotiation.
Knowledge as refusal to let the text
have the last word.

---

Here at the edge of the argument
I have written: *not yet.*

Not *no,* exactly.
Not the confident refusal.

Just: more is possible.
There is still something to say.
The margin is not full.
