**I.**

I write to you from a city that does not yet exist,
from a Tuesday in the middle of a year
no one will remember.

The light is the same light
that falls on every ordinary morning —
neither kind nor unkind,
a brightness that asks nothing.

---

**II.**

What was it you said
about the difference between
silence and silence?

I have been practicing both.
One is a room with no windows.
The other is a field.

---

**III.**

The letter I cannot send
grows heavier with each revision.
By now it weighs enough
to anchor a small vessel.

I fold it instead
into the shape of something
I haven't named yet —

a question,
a country,
a door.

---

**IV.**

There is a politics of the unsent.
Consider everything
that was never mailed,
the care that accumulated
in envelopes no one opened,
the grievances that calcified
into monuments no one visits.

---

**V.**

I address you now
without a name,
which is to say
I address everyone
and no one,
which is to say
I address the city,
the Tuesday,
the ordinary light.

I hope this reaches you.
I hope this reaches you.
I hope this reaches you.
