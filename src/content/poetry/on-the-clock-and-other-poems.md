**On the Clock**

I read *The Once and Future King*  
one summer on my bus rides  
to and from the wire works.  
The future king went flying with  
a flock of geese. He did—  
performed—those things reserved  
for kings. I ran my drill press  
faithfully as if I forged a shield,  
and punched the strict anachronistic clock  
that tallied up my time for my reward.  
Yet truly, I myself retrieved the sword.  
It’s all the same to me what you believe.

**Tweed Cap**

Bernard, my South Side uncle, drove a truck  
delivering Chicago’s wants and needs.  
I think he stashed a pistol underneath  
the driver’s seat but never brandished it.  
At twelve I showed him my new barbell set.  
He reached down with one arm and raised the bar  
and iron plates above his head. “Good weight,”  
he said. The smoothest lift they’d ever get.  
He’d listen to Claude Debussy up late  
and sleepless from his job. That blue guitar  
Picasso print hung on his wall. He’d struck  
a balance few achieve between hard-bit  
and “Clair de lune.” For this, one wins no wreath  
like those bestowed for gaudy words and deeds.

**An Olive or a Purple Haze**

<blockquote>
. . . a shagginess remains, / An olive or a purple haze, . . .
</blockquote>
<p class="poem-attr">—Donald Justice, in “Variations on a Theme from James,” 1960</p>

In sun the haze is olive green,  
in strobe light, purple.  
The handle of a palette knife  
leans half outside a coffee tin  
across the open lip of which  
a clutch of brushes lie  
still moist but drying.  
A studio’s a studio,  
the record’s clear on that score.  
Hearing colors, seeing sounds  
remains a pastime, old as  
handprints ochred on a limestone wall,  
as flutes of reed  
and horns of hollowed bone  
cut from the shaggy world  
and fashioned such that they must yield  
to practiced hands and breath  
a limpid timbre and a purer tone.

**Emoticon**

When Joffrey Stewart cried “de-MOCK-racy”  
he twanged pronunciation like steel wire.  
His sequel “DE-mo-CRAZY” let us see  
as well as hear the nation’s case was dire.  
We were of course, his audience, the choir,  
Chicago poets, drop-ins, dropouts, Vets  
for Peace, all passing through our phoenix fire,  
a red glow and blue haze of cigarettes.  
We’re circling in smoke rings now, our debts  
forgiven, paid in full, defaulted on,  
or slow-defrayed by lucky winning bets:  
Spare, if you can, a stray e-MOTE-i-CON!  
I hear you, Joffrey, strung along my nerves,  
give each new word the accent it deserves.

**The Making of Herdsmen**

<blockquote>
I oblivionize skyscrapers.
</blockquote>
<p class="poem-attr">—Harry Crosby, “Assassin”</p>

<div class="poem-alt-indent">
<p>Where can’t assassins feel inspired?</p>
<p>Young Crosby scraped up carnage at</p>
<p>Verdun. This gave him insight to</p>
<p>the workings of the Sun, a row</p>
<p>he plowed the rest of his brief life,</p>
<p>self-ended with a .25.</p>
<p>The fellows aiming aircraft at the towers</p>
<p>were surely unaware their concept had been scooped</p>
<p>by Boston-Harvard stock, a libertine</p>
<p>whose wife, Caresse, had named her whippet</p>
<p>Clitoris. The world is full of prophets,</p>
<p>though, as some assassins come to know,</p>
<p>and drop their weapons, take up shepherd’s crooks,</p>
<p>and underneath the Sun’s obliviousness,</p>
<p>tend their flocks and</p>
<p>watch wild lilies grow.</p>
</div>
