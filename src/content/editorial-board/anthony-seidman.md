shortBio: "Anthony Seidman is a poet and translator who has spent significant amounts of time living in and writing from Mexico's northern border. His full-length translations include *Contra Natura* by Rodolfo Hinostroza, *Smooth-Talking Dog* by Roberto Castillo Udiarte, *The End of the World Came to My Neighborhood* by Frank Báez, and *Confetti-Ash: Selected Poems of Salvador Novo*."
fullBio: "Juan Felipe Herrera claimed that his translation and unearthing of *A Stab in the Dark* by Facundo Bernal \"truly changes everything in Latinx literature.\" Seidman's translations, reviews, articles, and poems have appeared in such journals as Latin American Literature Today, The Black Herald, Los Angeles Review of Books, Huizache, World Literature Today, The Bitter Oleander, Modern Poetry in Translation, Review-Literature and Arts of the Americas, and New American Writing.\n\nHis work appears in anthologies like *Asymmetries: Contemporary Peruvian Poetry*, *The Ecopoetry Anthology*, *The Serpent and the Fire*, and *La vida en sí-Poesía de lo cotidiano en el norte de México*. His poetry has been translated into Spanish and French, and featured in journals from Argentina, Mexico, and Chile. With artist Jean-Claude Loubières, he has published four artist books through AdèLéo Press. The books are housed in such collections as the Bibliothèque Kandinsky at the Centre-Pompidou and Le Centre des livres d'artistes."
plate: "The Latin American Literature section in The Rang Yan Review seeks to discover and herald new and forgotten voices, as well as to continue the research and translation of the voices percieved as great from the past and those currently producing poetry, prose, and essays; however, no name is too great enough to not be questioned or re-examined from a new angle. We are particularly interested in marginal and vanguard voices, or literature produced from underlooked regions, yet of undeniable power and brilliance."
