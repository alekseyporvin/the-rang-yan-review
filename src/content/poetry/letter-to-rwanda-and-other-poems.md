**Letter to Rwanda**

<div class="poem-prose">
<p>In 1994 I was ten.</p>
<p>I remember being at my grandmother’s, on the other side of the courtyard.</p>
<p>She kept waffles in a dresser, rolled up like cigars.</p>
<p>There was the set of Ludo pawns, whittled from wood and painted by my grandfather. My grandmother was cheating.</p>
<p>There was a clock and a wardrobe that seemed to have been made by very large humans.</p>
<p>There was a television in the coldest corner of the house. Bruno was talking about Rwanda.</p>
<p>For several weeks, Bruno Masure talked about Rwanda.</p>
<p>Here it was the mildest time of year, the time of lizards, of riding my bike till dusk, soon school would be out.</p>
<p>I hear “Kigali”. Bruno Masure is worried. Many people are dying. I don’t know Africa. I’m afraid of Kigali, of red eyes and black gums.</p>
<p>I met Boris in a lift. In Italy. We exchanged a glance, a glance three-stories long.</p>
<p>In 1994, Boris was a journalist. Boris had been fooled then he opened his eyes and his eyelids were burnt.</p>
<p>Now Boris travels the world and opens its eyes.</p>
<div class="poem-inset">
<p>he’d seen the monster<br />
the one who shares our dreams<br />
beseeches our gods<br />
eats at our table<br />
loves those we love<br />
and weeps our own tears<br />
the monster within<br />
same body, same soul</p>
</div>
<p>Boris exposed the monster in a gospel of sulphur.</p>
<p>I met Bruce. He served me black tea from a yellow box with a landscape of lakes and volcanoes. He told me Rwanda was beautiful and very green.</p>
<p>August-September 1994, I’m gorging on plums. I’m thinking of the colour of my new pencil case for school.</p>
<p>Bruce is in Rwanda.</p>
<div class="poem-inset">
<p>he’s seen the monster<br />
he’s seen the white bodies<br />
a methodical line-up of skulls and shinbones<br />
some are no bigger than a doll</p>
</div>
<p>Bruce dressed them in flesh and colours and painted them upright.</p>
<p>I’ve read and seen what memory has patiently exhumed from the genocide.</p>
<p>That genocide, the one seen live on TV.</p>
<p>The monster is identical twenty years later. Only it has a few million new teeth.</p>
<p>There are innocents who believe he has crossed the border.</p>
<p>They’re the worst, the innocents who don’t know that the monster lives under their roof and wears their clothes, that the monster roams their body.</p>
<p>I have known since I was ten that the world flares up a little more each day and that new rains will smother its embers.</p>
<p>I know that other children will be ten and other children will die when they’re ten.</p>
</div>

<div class="poem-note">
<p>From <em>Connivences 4</em> (éditions de la Margeride, 2017).</p>
</div>

**Lady-flute’s cantilena**

I want you to hold me close  
In a chamber close to your heart  
I want your breath to flow through me

You will be the mouth and I the voice  
And together we’ll chant  
As fauns begin to dance

Come, with the tips of your fingers  
Obscure what inside of me  
Gives light to the silence

I want to slip inside your male lips  
To find myself captive  
And know you are gifted

I’ll teach you to read  
That other partition  
That until dawn  
Will see us falter

Provided you grant me  
Your coming night

<div class="poem-note">
<p>From <em>Un autre monde que le mien</em> (Éditions Bruno Doucey, 2025). Occitan title: <em>Sansoina de la femna-flaüta</em>.</p>
</div>

**In the palm of her hand**

What if the greatest gift life could offer  
Was to let us greet the child we once were  
To stroke her shoulder and to hold her close?

What if the last face to greet our eyes  
offered only the wonder of a looking glass  
reflecting the mask of our youth and the brightness of what is yet to be?  
What would we say to this child?  
Would we grow less fearful of the dark when we hear her sing a lullaby  
in her hesitant voice, reinventing words we’d forgotten?  
Can we even name the day we truly ceased to be that child who loved riding whales  
wondering why the Earth isn’t called Sea even though it’s covered in all that water?  
Did we stop communing with the plants in our garden  
or whispering to our pets  
the sweet words that gave us comfort?

Is this the reason why the language to linger last in our memory is also our first?  
To better welcome this child, her laughter and gesture when the warm palm of her hand, with infinite tenderness, comes to rest on our cold eyelids?

<div class="poem-note">
<p>After the painting <em>L’Aïeule</em> by Uranie Colin-Libour.</p>
<p>2020</p>
</div>

**Canto VI**

They came by sea in their small boats. They came with plaited beards and solemn eyes bearing news that a shepherd had stolen a queen. Ulysses joined them, weapons in hand, a smart helmet on his skull. But before setting off he made a pledge. May his soul be damned should he break his oath.

He says “return” she hears “departure”, he says “victory” she hears “loneliness”, he says “for ever” she answers “never”.

And so the old women take to wailing again.  
Terrified, the dogs gnaw at their rumps.  
She wraps herself in a shroud.

**She**

Give me a name, Ulysses

give me a name so that i can wait for you  
i’ll be here, the mirror, there  
and we’ll speak of you, i and the other in the mirror  
i’ll join her there, a little slant, on the edge of a chair, the way birds do  
the ache in my thigh keeps me from losing myself to that side of the mirror

in the morning i’ll wear my earrings  
i may even wear them to bed should you surprise me in the night

but if i have no name how will i know which of us, her or me, is waiting?

**Ulysses**

Give you a name?

Give you a name when you dance in the dark with great hounds in empty streets?

Give you a name when you stroll to the river dressed for night in the glaring sun, spurning  
the men who were doomed the moment they thought they possessed you?

I’ll offer you oranges  
and to peel them a knife no bigger than a thumb  
an ivory knife I’ll steal when the battle is over  
a dead man’s gift to another woman  
and you’ll be bound to think of her, of her cold sheets, of the hole in her pocket  
traded for the knife

I’ll offer you blades of grass that cling to the soles of my feet  
from shoots that grow there, where the bodies lie  
standing tall as sentinels at the precise point their retreat ended

**Canto VII**

There was a man. There was a woman,  
And after, within living memory, a myth was born.

The Tale begins on the first day of battle and follows the waterways of its return trajectory.

There will be other men and other stories.  
Other tales to the memory of a man named Ulysses.

**She**

i am here, alone again  
with this soft rain that insists on blending everything together  
on finding harmony in this hateful landscape

Ulysses exits

the stage is deserted  
the extras are consigned to the cellar, along with the set

the poet crosses this out…  
what’s missing is a woman to wait for Ulysses  
a woman, a dog  
and men  
to surround and to court her

**It all seems chastened**

the sun  
void

i built a labyrinth for the sun  
there’s a bull  
no, not that one  
a small rust-tinged bull, with eyes like marbles  
he’s the sun, poor little blind beast

and if the sun should sing  
the birds, their wings would burn to cinders

**There are all manner of sorrows**

water pouring from this damned basin  
caresses ordained by the gesture  
the perennial pleasure of warm water  
letting yourself be captured  
in the way that water catches the light  
finding rapture in bubbles  
even as the tears, the tears are falling  
bubbles, warmth, creases on the back of my hand  
all calling for a truce

here, now  
a truce

but your name comes back to me  
and i want to bury myself in this pool of water

i wear a bracelet on each ankle  
you could say i do more or less as i please  
no man, no name, no son  
alone as i am, i am the queen

yes, that’s what he used to call me, Ulysses  
that’s my lot  
she’s sort of all me, the queen  
the one whose turn it is to speak  
she has a king  
he’s often far from home, with his horses  
no, not the little mountain horses  
no, tall horses they took from the stonemasons  
with massive jaws and thick necks to bear the weight of their heads

there it is  
the walls are steep  
dancing surrounds her, but she will not dance

there is no music, no voice other than mine  
yes, i do have a mirror but i lied, i don’t want to see myself in it  
i may turn it towards the sky  
or take my leave one day  
and prop it there, staring straight at the door
