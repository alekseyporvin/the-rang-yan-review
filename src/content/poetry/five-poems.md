<pre class="poem-lines">No
poem
is made of truth.
Mine,
for example,
are made of more complex materials:
a lacerated chest,
requited love,
dehumanization,
anger,
melancholy,
her eyes,
late afternoons,
doubts,
etc.
Everything and a little more,
at an indescribable speed
that weakens me,
sometimes the body,
sometimes the mind,
sometimes the soul,
which needs stitching
with fine thread,
soaked
in my instantaneous truths.</pre>

<hr>

<pre class="poem-lines">When love
does not love you
out loud,
ask it to leave.
When love
finds it easier
to praise other women
for the smallest things they do
and stereotype you over the little things,
walk away.
Love is not a state of disarray.
Sew your chest back together
and write
what love does not want to hear,
but pretends to.</pre>

<hr>

<pre class="poem-lines">My permanent space
is not the kitchen.
I know how to nurture friendships,
but that is not servitude.
And if it is, I say,
there is no room in my chest for the plantations,
nor for the <em>sinhá</em>,
nor for the <em>sinhô</em>.
My body becomes a carnival,
and without irony I say:
Do not treat me badly
if you cannot treat me as an equal.</pre>

<div class="poem-note">
<p><em>Translator’s note:</em> The Portuguese terms <em>sinhá</em> and <em>sinhô</em> are historically charged forms of address for enslavers or masters and mistresses in Brazil. The word <em>engenhos</em> refers to the sugar plantations and mills associated with slavery. These terms evoke the legacy of slavery and the racialized structures of servitude.</p>
</div>

<pre class="poem-lines">No one taught me how to love;
love, to me,
is an unfamiliar metaphor,
one that does not acknowledge me publicly
or hold my hand.
Love does not take pleasure in me,
does not declare anything to me
day in and day out.
“Love” gives me bouquets of flowers from Chernobyl,
has a thousand profiles
that seem like a mirage
of what I need and what my body asks of me.
If love were
a hundred-year-old woman,
I would ask her to marry me,
shocking the entire social order
with a belated love
upon the soft skin of time
that never caressed me.</pre>

<hr>

<pre class="poem-lines">I need to write with affection,
but with the same hands I write with,
I pull the pin,
trigger the malfunction,
blow apart images,
discourses,
and ideas
created about me
at the expense of my silence.
I need to say that I know how to touch what is tender,
that I know how to kiss deliciously,
that I experience pleasure and feel
everything around me.
I am not flesh,
I am not a part,
I am triple: blood and synapses.
Sometimes I am dizziness itself,
I live in the silence
of an indescribable murmuring sea.
Sometimes I am the irreversible mold
of the Nation’s history:
I devour the national archive
and defecate pesticides
and exploitation.
I need to write
with my own hands,
my own eyes,
and my own tears.
Put away the white erasers,
the correction fluid,
and the “delete” key.
I write in this language
because other languages were stolen from me
and this one was imposed upon me.</pre>
