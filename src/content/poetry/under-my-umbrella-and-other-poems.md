**Under My Umbrella**

Hunkering under my umbrella  
while the sky wrings itself dry  
I feel absent of body, not mind.  
My step is mechanical, and moves  
along a virtual assembly line.  
Parts grind, gnash, hum and skip  
a beat or two. The houses

I pass look curious as cats.  
Their windows open onto souls  
drinking coffee, reading the news  
on the family laptop, or dreaming  
of a thrilling moment of incest  
that luckily will never happen.  
Yet the shock transmits to me

and ugly knotted pictures form.  
I don’t want to see so I tip  
the umbrella and spritz myself  
with the purest natural solvent.  
Why would I attribute horrors  
to neighbors who’ve done no harm  
even in the collective dark?

The shade my umbrella casts  
shelters many species of thought,  
a few possibly fatal to peace  
of mind. The sky is almost done  
with itself. I fold the umbrella  
and allow myself to clarify  
in the cloudy but purified light.

**Musical Treetops**

Sounds of trumpets and tubas  
in the pines this morning:  
the collective effect mocks  
the jazz to which I aspired  
with a crude touch on the piano.

I was a lean but fragile  
adolescent. I drove myself  
to the nether parts of the city  
to listen to instruments tune up  
and watch musicians turn on

with spikes of luxury drugs.  
They never concealed themselves  
from this pale, attenuated boy  
peering through opaque glasses  
and listening with opaque mind.

Now the brass raves in treetops  
and pianos are digital keyboards  
that moan like windy ghosts.  
The drummers marched away,  
abandoning their kits in the street.

Alto mates with tenor sax  
and jazz still streams in the ether  
though no one hears it cohere  
in coughing fits of cigarettes  
and splashes of unruly bourbon.

I stay home these days, the clubs  
closed forever and the big streets  
panting with tuneless anger.  
The pines play adequate music,  
from which I can still learn to live.

**Valley Mist**

Dream-worlds tied in bundles  
like so many dead, dry sticks,  
fuel to burn myself at the stake.

Some would call them fasces,  
but I’m not engaged in politics  
on this shaggy wet morning.

I press myself against myself  
and an ooze of excess fluids  
stains my last expensive shirt.

The mist rising from the valley  
cloaks my backyard in the grace  
no one either earns or refuses.

I should march down to the rally  
and take a side and placard it  
and pump myself full of virtue.

The horizon folds over itself,  
expecting gray shadows of rain.  
Standing by the roadside holding

a sign reading “No Kings Today”  
I’ll risk weather for a precept  
schoolchildren already know.

But that will happen at midday  
when my dislike of politics fades  
and the valley mist clears for

a brevity of fellowship,  
passing cars honking thumbs-up,  
a few rough shouts of derision.

**Low Spots**

The obtuse slope of this village  
tips us into the low spots  
where we slog through aster

and goldenrod, picking up ticks  
bearing wretched brown disease.  
We can’t walk on pavement because

the realtors claim the frontage  
and lawyers parade in gray suits  
working people can’t afford,

even for Sunday best when  
the Episcopal church yawns  
and accepts their meager offerings.

The low spots border a river  
that snores with modest current.  
Sometimes we fish for bluegills

and fry them as tasty white slabs  
dusted with cornmeal and washed down  
with the cheapest supermarket beer.

We allow ourselves to climb  
from the muck to the library  
where the death of literacy

prevails in rusty shades of black.  
As we paw through old magazines  
the river mud exudes the ghosts

of our dead high-school comrades,  
their faces like those of the small fry  
caught swimming against the flow.
