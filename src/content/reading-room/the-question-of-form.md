Form is not a container for content. This is the first thing to say, and perhaps the most important. The relationship between how something is written and what it means is not that of bowl to water — where the bowl is neutral, interchangeable, and the water remains the same regardless of what holds it.

Form is meaning. The sentence that piles clause upon clause, stretching toward a horizon it cannot quite reach, is saying something different from the sentence that stops short. The essay that circles back, that contradicts itself, that refuses resolution — it is making an argument that the linear essay, however sophisticated its propositions, cannot make.

## Why Form Is Political

If form is meaning, then choices about form are not aesthetic choices in some narrow sense — a matter of taste, of style, of personal preference. They are choices about how to represent reality, about what reality looks like, about what relationships are possible between a writer and a reader.

The form that presents itself as transparent — as simply delivering content without interference — is making the most loaded formal claim of all: that there is content that exists prior to and independent of its presentation, that language is a neutral medium, that the reader and writer occupy equivalent positions. None of this is true.

A committed transparency about the constructed nature of the text, about the choices that went into making it, about the impossibility of a view from nowhere — this is not obscurantism. It is honesty.

## On the Fragment

The fragment has a particular relevance here. The form that presents itself as incomplete — that does not promise resolution, that acknowledges its own limitations, that leaves space for the reader to do work — is enacting a set of values that the complete, finished, authoritative text cannot. It says: this is not the whole truth. There is more to say. You are not simply a receiver of what I have worked out; you are a participant in a thinking that is not finished.

The fragment is not a failure of the whole. It is a different kind of completion.

I offer these notes as fragments. I do not know where the argument ends. I am not sure it does.
