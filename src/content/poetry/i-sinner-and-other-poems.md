<p><strong>I, Sinner</strong></p>

<p>after Elvira Hernández</p>

<div class="burghelea">
<p>- i confess to placating desire</p>
<p class="burghelea-in">before running fingers across its knife-edged mouth,</p>
<p>- succumb to its sizzling pace</p>
<p class="burghelea-in">the way it dries up skin and mind alike</p>
<p>- these days, it requires a verifiable identity,</p>
<p class="burghelea-in">before it gets a fix, brief and ripe</p>
<p>- the flutter in the lower abdomen, licking blood from a fresh cut,</p>
<p class="burghelea-in">knowing there is still a pint of lemon gelato in the fridge,</p>
<p>- grackles emptying their throats in April, Bob Dylan’s raspy voice in Red River Shore,</p>
<p class="burghelea-in">a stranger’s humming, then silence</p>
<p>- i confess to lying as flirt, repetition, loss, comfort,</p>
<p class="burghelea-in">half-truths, interruptions, survival</p>
<p>- i confess to being a poor holder of other people’s lies</p>
<p class="burghelea-in">we touch what we can hardly touch</p>
<pre class="poem-lines">absent&#9;&#9;fatigued&#9;&#9;impulsive
unattached       organized&#9;            halfway</pre>
<p>- i confess to these and words as swallows, fake dreams, light splinters,</p>
<p class="burghelea-in">dead mother, chance and choice, innuendos</p>
<p>- to have the reader invent and discover their own shadow</p>
<p class="burghelea-in">then follow through</p>
<p>- to the self-erasing past catching up on its breath</p>
<p class="burghelea-in">before it whispers to keep writing</p>
<p>- to English as a tall lover who denies me (/θ/, /ð/) and (/ɪ/, /æ/, /ʊ/)</p>
<p class="burghelea-in">has me boxed and framed before breathing life into my clay tongue.</p>
</div>

<p class="poem-next"><strong>The maybes are the hardest thing</strong></p>

<div class="poem-prose">
<p>One day it felt I chewed out all the anger on bread rolls, gnarls in the crust, in the heart, then lay down my tongue, ripe with blisters yet freed from the whorl of hunger so there I was, soft as the pull cord of the window blind skittering the dusty floor, I guess it happens as we sit down a story and unravel the delight that sugarcoats a <em>first</em>, later the self-devouring inattention to things unrelated to its plenty, it doesn’t matter as long as we trot together its avenues of splendor, stubble sprouting like fine bristles on the face of a stranger on the C train, before we know it, the remarkable becomes the stuff of ridicule, heart and lungs rush into stillness, the desire behind desire takes hold, morphing into resentment, perhaps what matters is to be half-waking from the half-dream, swallow the bitter on the tongue, watch the world heavy among the branches.</p>
</div>

<p class="poem-next"><strong>Upon having finished reading <em>Sagawa Chika Translations, Anti-Translations &amp; Originals</em></strong></p>

<div class="poem-prose">
<p>There are three key psychological facts to consider about Sawako Nakayasu’s <em>Mouth: Eats Color</em>:</p>
<p>(1) There is a shapeshifting, language-shedding, mouth-watering flavor of her “Promenade” poems in translation.</p>
<p>(2) Like the survivor of a game of Russian roulette, the poet gambles words, and languages alike. She gets lost and lost and lost. She blackens and whitens the lines. Her mouth spills out flower petals and sensory distortions. The reader is part of this endless devouring.</p>
<p>(3) The infra-textual work performs its playfulness in plain sight. If confused, you should consider the haikus:</p>
</div>

<pre class="poem-lines">Honeysuckle crouched
beside the road, unblooming,
the translation bleeds.

A flock of snow sans
promise, fish climb tall river,
then, ouvre ta bouche.</pre>

<p class="poem-next"><strong>Something is always spilling</strong></p>

<pre class="poem-lines">a chest, a cut of earth, a slither of light, a barren tongue,
a blistered dream, the thick belly of summer, a pregnant
shadow, a wronged womb, a pair of plucking hands, a belated
myth, your name in another man’s mouth, a see-through truth,
next, a pocket of dark, there will always be a worse version of us
living on the margins, waiting-rooming for the undreamt, white
noise slapping against the window, lend me your retina to lick it
goodnight, roll all vile tears on my wicked lips, skin loosening.</pre>

<p class="poem-next"><strong>On the extraordinary</strong></p>

<div class="poem-prose">
<p>My friend’s succulents outlive her by ten days, each morning her ghost thickens in me like sap on a sweetgum tree, the splinter in my thumb works its way out of the flesh, a pair of cardinals share my bench in Highland Park, morning rolls up its sleeves and delivers the much-wanted blood, I survive the same lie twice, the soft meat of tomorrow thaws into my wicked mouth, the wet of it reeking of fear, my nights, too long, my dreams, riddles begging to be solved, on the page I nurse rows of words, milkless, skinned, how many times have you said my name to yourself, each spell, the echo tattoos the air, I can hear all darkness seethe inside, yours forever.</p>
</div>
