excerpt: "The archive of war speaks through a politics of selective visibility: as language recedes into whiteness, the surviving words become fragments of an ideological and emotional apparatus through which private memory, imperial allegiance, and the moral economy of sacrifice are made legible. By exposing the violence of erasure as an aesthetic operation, the work interrogates the historical production of remembrance itself, asking whose voices remain audible, whose suffering is rendered peripheral, and how the intimate correspondence of individuals becomes entangled in the grand narratives of empire and war."

<p class="ghadery-p ghadery-bold">Dear Mum and Dad</p>
<p class="ghadery-p">Remembrance Day 2026</p>
<p class="ghadery-p"><span class="ghadery-pale">The days seem to move along at their own pace. Some days fly by, and others creep along. We are officially at the halfway point </span>no<span class="ghadery-pale">w</span><span class="ghadery-mist">, </span>t<span class="ghadery-pale">hough I can’t believe I’ve been here for three months. I try to remind myself to appreciate every experience – even the ones I don’t really enjoy. :)</span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p"><span class="ghadery-pale">I have been thinking a lot about </span>fate <span class="ghadery-pale">lately</span>. It was<span class="ghadery-mist"> </span><span class="ghadery-pale">such an accident of birth that we ended up where we did when we did, that we are where we are now, with the </span>choice<span class="ghadery-pale">s that we have available to us. It seems to me that we have such a burden of responsibility to make the world a better place for those who were born into far worse circumstances. It is more than donating money to charities; it is taking action and trying to make things better. You have both shown me that </span>throughout my life, <span class="ghadery-pale">but here I realize it more th</span>a<span class="ghadery-pale">n ever.</span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p"><span class="ghadery-pale">My </span>current<span class="ghadery-pale"> job and role in Afghanistan is part of that, but it is more the non-governmental organizations that come later. They are the ones that really make the difference. I like to think that my being here means they will be able to come that much sooner and operate more freely. I will be looking for more opportunities to volunteer in Wainwright (Alberta) and to really try to make a difference. It is humbling to be here, part of something so much bigger than myself.</span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p"><span class="ghadery-pale">Love always</span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p ghadery-gap"><span class="ghadery-pale">Dear Mother and all,</span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p ghadery-gap"><span class="ghadery-pale">       Well as usual there is not much to write about.  I note that I have not received a letter from you for about two to three weeks but I know that you’r [sic] all well or I would have heard from you.</span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p ghadery-gap"><span class="ghadery-pale">  It rained nearly all last week so we did not do much out door work but fooled around in side just putting in time.</span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p ghadery-gap"><span class="ghadery-pale"> The 62nd were to leave to-day but a wire came through about 8 o'clock last night post-poning it and you may say there were some </span>wild<span class="ghadery-pale"> men in town.  We were </span>as<span class="ghadery-pale">ked to come in Saturday </span>night<span class="ghadery-pale"> so </span>as <span class="ghadery-pale">to be on hand to give them a send off.  So I guess there will be some sore 72nd men coming back and finding </span>the boys <span class="ghadery-pale">were not going.</span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p ghadery-gap"><span class="ghadery-pale">  Do you k</span>now<span class="ghadery-pale"> Allan Davidson of Westbank?  Well I guess he will be glad the 62nd did not get away.  For he is in the Hospital and has just under </span>gone <span class="ghadery-pale">an operation so if they stay for a few weeks he will be able to get away with them.</span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p ghadery-gap"><span class="ghadery-pale"> Oh! I say John I hear that you have cut Rev Marshall out. Congratulations.  I hope it is true.  I got that information from an ex Peachlander.</span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p ghadery-gap"><span class="ghadery-pale"> As you see by the heading of the paper I am writing this letter down in the Khaki Club.  It is a very nice little place fixed up down stairs in the Standard Bank Building with a reading room with easy chairs and a writing room.  Say did you get the ten dollar money order OK?  Hoping you are all well.  I am</span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p ghadery-gap"><span class="ghadery-pale">          Your Loving Son</span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p ghadery-gap"><span class="ghadery-pale">                       G</span>or<span class="ghadery-pale">don</span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p"><span class="ghadery-pale">Dear Mother and all – </span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p"><span class="ghadery-pale">I received your ever welcome letter of May 27 last night, was glad to hear that you are all well. I am myself in the very best of health, in fact I never felt better in my life. We are having ideal weather, for a few days it was very hot, but we had a thunderstorm the other day and it has not been so hot since. Cherries and early </span>berries <span class="ghadery-pale">are now getting </span>ripe and<span class="ghadery-pale"> we are getting our share of them. At present we are camped in an orchard supposed to be resting; we are only a few miles from where the British are bombarding a </span>large<span class="ghadery-pale"> town which has for months been a German stronghold. The roar of the big guns is continual and is about the only thing that reminds us that there is a war. In a few days we expect to be back at it again. Sooner or later Germany must give way, before the ever increasing forces that are against her, but they are going to hold on longer than some people think, most of her troops out here don’t think that we will see another winter campaign, but personally I think we will. I read with interest the clipping contained in Glen’s letter. I knew he was wounded but did not know how bad, w</span>as <span class="ghadery-pale">glad to know that he got off so lucky as a clean </span>bullet wound <span class="ghadery-pale">i</span>s <span class="ghadery-pale">nothing serious, if it is not in a </span>vital<span class="ghadery-pale"> spot, as an army bullet is small and hard as steel, but a fragment of a shell is the worst as it is jagged and all shapes. We had a sergeant, a nice young fellow, married, with one child, who got hit in the leg with a piece of shrapnel; he did not think it w</span>as<span class="ghadery-pale"> very bad, but I guess the </span>bone<span class="ghadery-pale"> must have been shattered as they had to amputate the limb and he died – but a bullet if it goes through is </span>no<span class="ghadery-pale">t very bad. I have seen thousands of wounded men, some of them were remarkable escapes from death, but the worst wrecks seem to be those who get a bad dose of gas, and that stuff will make your eyes smart and run water, a couple of miles from where it is being used. Well, </span>I have never been sorry <span class="ghadery-pale">for coming here. I have had good health, we get lots of food and clothes and see some wonderful sights. Sights that man will never see again, at times our dangers are as great as any unit in the army and our task just as important, but our food is better than some other corps, while we have a little better chance of keeping clear and better attendance if sick. I have not seen any Germans at close quarters, only prisoners, but have several times seen men killed and wounded by their fire, but I have every </span>hope <span class="ghadery-pale">and firmly believe that I will be safely permitted to return to dear old Canada, the best country in the world. In the Canadian papers we read a lot about elections and the third and fourth contingent with the war news that </span>is a<span class="ghadery-pale">bout all they contain. You people at home get more news of the war as a whole sooner than we, who are engaged in it, but some day the pages of history that are now being made will be written and those of us who are left will be able to read the chapters of glory of which the volunteers of the mighty British Empire will be the aut</span>h<span class="ghadery-pale">ors inst</span>ea<span class="ghadery-pale">d of the consc</span>r<span class="ghadery-pale">ip</span>t<span class="ghadery-pale"> hordes – of Germany and our country shall be free. </span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p"><span class="ghadery-pale">   Well, I gues</span>s t<span class="ghadery-pale">his letter w</span>ill<span class="ghadery-pale"> </span>be<span class="ghadery-pale"> long enough to try the p</span>at<span class="ghadery-pale">ience of the censor if he reads it, so I had better close for this time. </span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p"><span class="ghadery-pale">   Your lov</span>ing<span class="ghadery-pale"> son, </span></p>
<p class="ghadery-p ghadery-gap"><span class="ghadery-pale">W.J. McMAHAN</span></p>
