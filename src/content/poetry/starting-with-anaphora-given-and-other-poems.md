<p class="bett-head bett-head--first"><strong>Starting with Anaphora: </strong><span class="bett-sub"><strong>Given (Étant donnés, 1946-66)</strong></span></p>

<div class="bett-note">
<p>“Étant donnés (Given: 1. The Waterfall, 2. The Illuminating Gas)<em> is a 1966 </em><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assemblage_(art)"><em>assemblage</em></a><em>. It was Duchamp’s last major artwork, surprising viewers and critics who had widely believed he had given up art; he was previously pursuing competitive chess which he had been playing for almost 25 years. The artwork is a </em><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tableau_(sculpture)"><em>tableau</em></a><em>, visible only through a pair of peepholes—one for each eye—in a wooden door, of a nude woman lying on her back [on a bed of dry leaves &amp; twigs] on a hill with her face hidden, legs spread, holding a gas lamp in the air in one hand against a landscape backdrop [of forest &amp; waterfall].</em></p>
<p><em>Duchamp worked in secrecy on the artwork from 1946 to 1966 in his </em><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Village"><em>Greenwich Village</em></a><em> studio. It is composed of an old wooden door, nails, bricks, brass, aluminium sheet, steel binder clips, velvet, leaves, twigs, a female form made of parchment, hair, glass, plastic clothespins, oil paint, linoleum, an assortment of lights, a landscape composed of hand-painted and photographed elements and an electric motor housed in a cookie tin which rotates a perforated disc to create a waterfall effect.” </em></p>
</div>

forbiddance explicit ABS no RE<strong>∙</strong>productioned gratis

<p class="bett-head"><strong>To Be Ashamed: </strong><span class="bett-sub"><strong>Given 2</strong></span></p>

<pre class="poem-lines poem-lines--bett">She aint on a pedestal, she’s on a pyre       Rready to burn

Our eyes burnt too, fixed to smoldering peepholes

From glassy Bride to this heedless headless <em>Given</em> grrl
Who’s projecting who?

&amp; where’s Marcel behind us pulling strings, playing
chess through all this quiet mayhem?

Where does his exhibition open &amp; our exhibitionist
run out of gaslight?

Then there’s the matter (should we say <em>matériel</em>?)
sticking pig hair on her pubes
to get that ol’ <em>trompe d’oeil</em> effect
(remember <em>pudenda</em>: thing to be ashamed of)

The Duch im<strong>∙</strong>pudent       right to the end’a

He’s sure tromped on our eye
that’s a <em>given</em></pre>

<p class="bett-head"><strong>Three Wags &amp; A Tale: </strong><span class="bett-sub"><strong>Given 3</strong></span></p>

<pre class="poem-lines poem-lines--bett">One sharp wag at the <em>Times</em> quips, with ref 
to nature’s art —     this bizarre assemblage’s 
<em>apotheosis &amp; farewell in one</em>

                             … just as we’ve long said g’bye pls storytime 
                             PoWorld narrative poems gone done shelf life 
                             (adios, brutha sista poetasters)

A second wag observes, whence irony &amp; 
subjectivity the axis of Mod Art thence 
our D makes such axis spin on itself
“His laughing picture [sic] laughs at us”

So who’s projecting here?         one stripped Bride’s 
decoded, her heedless heir grrl Dee’s denuded

A third wag quotes Duch best
<em>There is no solution, because there is no problem.</em>

Case of tale wagging dog?</pre>
