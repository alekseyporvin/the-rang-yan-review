There is a particular kind of reading that happens in difficult times — a reading that is also reconnaissance, a scanning of the text for resources, for tools, for something that might prove useful in conditions the writer could not have anticipated. I have been reading Gramsci this way.

The Prison Notebooks are not easy reading under any conditions. They were written under conditions of extreme constraint — physical, temporal, material — and they bear the marks of that constraint: fragmentary, incomplete, circling back, leaving arguments without conclusions. Gramsci wrote in code to evade censorship, used philosophical language to mean political things. The result is dense and requiring.

## Hegemony as Cultural Work

The concept that comes back to me most insistently is hegemony — not in the debased form it has acquired in some contemporary usages, where it means simply "dominance," but in Gramsci's more precise and demanding sense: the way in which a ruling class maintains its position not only through coercion but through the active winning of consent, the naturalization of its values, the organization of common sense in ways that make the existing order feel not like a historical arrangement but like a feature of reality.

What this requires, on Gramsci's account, is cultural work. The institutions through which a society understands itself — schools, churches, media, political parties, intellectual life — are not neutral transmitters of information. They are sites of struggle over what is thinkable, what is desirable, what is possible. To contest hegemony is to contest these institutions, or to build counter-institutions, or both.

## The Organic Intellectual

Gramsci's concept of the "organic intellectual" — the person who emerges from a class or social group and articulates its worldview, its aspirations, its self-understanding — has sometimes been sentimentalized in ways he would not have recognized. The organic intellectual is not a heroic figure speaking truth to power from outside the system. She is someone doing the slow, patient, difficult work of building the cultural infrastructure within which a different politics becomes possible.

This is unglamorous work. It looks like editing a journal, organizing a reading group, writing for an audience that may never respond, maintaining relationships of trust and solidarity across difference and time. It looks like the work we are trying to do here.

I am not sure it is enough. But I am not sure what "enough" would mean in a moment like this one. The work is the work.
