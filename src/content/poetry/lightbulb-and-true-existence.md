<p><strong>Lightbulb<br />電球</strong></p>

<pre class="poem-lines poem-lines--arai">In the cold of winter, a camellia blooms, head hanging low. Watching, there in the doorframe, a lady, all lipsticked and ready.
“Say, mind changing a lightbulb for me?”
Our gazes briefly meet. Doesn’t sound like she’s from around here. I step up, take off my shabby shoes, embarrassed. “Oh my, we’ve got the same socks!” Her strangely clear voice sends a shiver down my spine. I hear the floorboards creak.
The cedar ceiling’s absurdly high, much too tall for me. I search for a stepladder and come back to find her—<span class="poem-shift">Standing there,</span><span class="poem-shift">Her middle-aged body</span><span class="poem-shift">Clad only in a glossy, scarlet underrobe</span>In the pale light through the shoji screen, she’s looking down, fingering the cord around her waist, bare feet standing on the muslin kimono she’s just shed—
<em>(She must be out of her mind)</em>
Needless to say, I drop the ladder and turn to go.

“Please, I’m not going to do anything
I just want someone to see”
She casts her voice out, chasing after me. Delicate and thin, its trail grows sharper still, a hook catching deep in my ear. She is breathing heavily now. <em>Aaaah—aaah—aaah—aaah</em>, her breath grows wild, panting, smoldering, foul-smelling, I can’t breathe, I’m suffocating—
I can’t help but look. Our eyes meet and—
I see her make-up has run
Revealing the clear, pure face beneath
Fine—demons, snakes, whatever—I’ll take them on. I ready myself, push her down, pull off her robe, but oh my—what do I find?
Another layer underneath
“I just want you to look—that’s all.”
In that second white underrobe, she shifts, thighs rubbing, fingers fiddling with the hem. Looks like she’s just died, wrapped a shroud—her forehead wrinkles smoothed, her face waxen and cool, her eyes deep with sudden flame. Enraptured, I grab her collar, and pull it down when—
It wasn’t there
Her breast was gone
Across the flat surface, smooth as snow, Crawls a single centipede, “I had surgery twenty years ago”

<em>I had surgery
twenty years ago
aaah—aaah—aaah—aaah
you look down on me, the sea of my chest undulating, heaving, aaah—aaah—aaah—aaah, bright red, the scar over my heart, swelling up like it was just born, just now
my breast, a scorpion, aaah—aaah, these sutures, scissor-shaped claws, the tools that gouged out these deep scars, the tail of the constellation Scorpio—want me to breath deep? it rises up, filled with breath, aaah—aaah
aaah—aaah
that morning
a camellia was blooming
a single flower in the hospital hedge
I wore a white robe, the doctor had a nose a lot like yours
the anesthetic was working, I was in a fog when a voice found my lost ears
“we’re starting now”
afraid, I forced my lids open
the brightly burning lightbulb still burns in my eyes, in my reflection I see in hell’s waters
turn it on again
quick this time
the doctor that looked like you is here again today, I’m in my white robe
blooming on the hedge
turn it on, come now
you grazed me, yes, you did, your scissors, so cold
I’ll give you undulations that grow bigger and bigger
my chest, burning bright with fever
me always in my white robe, you always the doctor
you cut me open, didn’t you?
you cut the white camellia out front
that’s why the red kimono poured forth!
why haven’t you turned me on yet?!
the lightbulb, I mean
poisoned needles prying me apart
clawing at me, opening my eyes and my body
sticking into me, covering me
here quick
turn it on
it’ll be nice, here in the haze of the anesthetic, so nice and cool
I’ll burst and pull in breath
my chest will swell up and up and up and up—does it disgust you?
reach down and pull me up
flowers bloom best on the hedge
doesn’t matter if they’re red or white
I’m the lightbulb
blowing in the breeze
I’m a round lantern
a star formed from sour night-dew
climb up, climb up this thread, climb up the suture you snap off in your teeth
Scorpio hangs in the sky, suspended,
bound up in countless threads
a great scorpion on my swollen stomach
reflected here in
this handheld mirror

oh lightbulb!
lightbulb

lightbulb, time to turn it on!</em></pre>

<p class="poem-next"><strong>True Existence<br />実存</strong></p>

<pre class="poem-lines poem-lines--arai"><span class="poem-shift-2"><em>Which one is the girl you want? One monme’s worth of flowers</em></span><span class="poem-shift-2"><em>That one is the girl we want. One monme’s worth of flowers</em></span>
Before anyone knew for sure the girl was even here, she’d been endowed with life. Long before. Still in her glass altar, she’d been defrosted in the microwave—the kiss to seal the oath. Wasn’t the matchmaker who smelled of disinfectant in fact a white-robed angel granting her life?
<span class="poem-shift">When one has eyes to see small, small, small things,</span>Existence also
Becomes so small, small, small.

You’re almost six feet tall, you say? The same size as an old-time yardstick. Now you’re only sixty microns. Please write in big block letters, write in permanent ink—you probably heard our wishes countless times when the donor was writing his name on the sperm collection cup. Even a white-robed angel can’t see the faces of us little tadpoles without putting on her reading glasses.
Will things turn out all right or not?—Fate is fifty-fifty. This or that? Fifty-fifty.
Really interesting. Even in our information society, what happens right next door is still a question of symbols.
<span class="poem-shift">When one has eyes to see small, small, small things,</span>Existence also
Becomes so big, big, big.

Am I really me? The idea we each have a mind that exists beyond matter is probably just a fantasy cooked up by monotheist padres. In this cup, there are two hundred million mes—me, me, me! In material terms, there’s more me’s here than the entire population of the Japanese archipelago. I could make my own Great Imperial Empire all by myself! I could cause a revolution at any moment! The possibilities are enormous! All we’d have to do is tap all these sleeping kids and wake them up.
Break the freezer lid, turn the battleship march up as loud as it’ll go.
The tadpoles would start springing out, one after another—Jumping, jumping, jumping, jumping. Like hitting it big in pachinko. Congratulations!
I swim four millimeters per minute, that’s how the angel appraised me—
I’ll be a real lady killer, knocking up the widows!
<span class="poem-shift"><em>We’re glad she lost, one monme’s worth of flowers</em></span><span class="poem-shift"><em>We’re glad we mock, one monme’s worth of flowers</em></span>
Let’s try again: Paper-scissors-rock.
One in two hundred million—Me, me, me, we all say
Three midwives hear—Me, me, me, oh-no, we all say
We sperm bet our lives on the order determined by the game
<span class="poem-shift">Running around</span><span class="poem-shift">In the dish, our eyes still can’t see</span><span class="poem-shift">Let’s try again: Paper-scissors-rock</span><span class="poem-shift">This is what we call “life” in the dish</span><span class="poem-shift">At least until we float belly up</span>
You know, the world of your existence
Is a world in which we’re already dead</pre>
